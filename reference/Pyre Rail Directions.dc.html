Pyre · the phase past overdue · v1.0
Two weeks overdue, more fire is no longer information — everything at the top of the rail would just be orange. So the sixth phase goes the other way: the fire is the loudest thing you can build, and past it the only louder move is for the fire to stop. Both options below sit at the same burn depth as overdue; they differ in whether the note is still alight.

Snooze does not reset the deadline, it damps the fire. The front holds where it was, the blob row drops to three low embers breathing on a 5.5s cycle, the flash and backglow shrink to a sixth, and a thin wisp lifts every twelve seconds. It reads as banked — alive, deliberately quieted, and obviously going to come back.
How the sixth phase behaves
trigger — heat > 1.20, i.e. roughly 7 days past due. Below that, overdue keeps its flash.
--burn stops — clamped at 30%. The comment must survive; a note you can no longer read is a note you cannot act on.
chroma leaves — the ignition ramp fades out over 4s on entry and a grey veil fades in at z-index 99. 9A takes it to zero; 9B keeps 3 dull embers.
flash stops — warmFlashHot is removed, not slowed. The absence of a heartbeat is the signal.
stock cools — #FBFAF6 → #E4E0D8 over 4s, ink drops to #4A443E, whole card to 90% opacity.
shake — 0.6px, 140ms, once every 9s, per §5.6. The only movement left.
sort — still top of the rail, but the eye now finds it by contrast rather than brightness.
reversible — editing the due date runs the entry animation backwards over 600ms and the note reignites.
Banking a note (9C)
what it is — snooze without lying: the due date is untouched, the countdown keeps counting, only the fire is damped. A banked note cannot hide a real deadline from you.
damping — flash and backglow to 1/6 strength, blob row to three low embers on a 5.5s breathe, wash to .16, --burn frozen at its current value.
entering — 900ms ease-out: the flash finishes its beat, then everything settles down together. No snap.
the label — a BANKED chip sits at the right of the subject row and the live countdown moves in beside the topic, so the damping and the real deadline are both readable at a glance. Hovering the chip shows the remaining bank time.
waking — at expiry the fire climbs back over 1.2s and the flash resumes on the beat it left. Re-banking the same note is allowed twice; a third attempt offers the edit sheet instead.
keys — B banks for 2h, shift-B for the rest of the day. Sort order does not change; a banked note keeps its place at the top.
Pyre · gradual burn system · v0.9
The five stages below are samples of one continuous variable, not five states. Everything — mask front, ember line, hotspots, glow, bloom — is driven off a single registered custom property --burn, so the note burns gradually and any two stages interpolate for free. Both cards here are live.

The model
one driver — @property --burn, syntax <percentage>, inherits: true, animatable. heat maps to it linearly: burn = clamp(0, heat × 26%, 26%).
everything reads it — mask front at var(--burn), each bite's height at calc(var(--burn) × 1.1–1.9), ember line stops at calc(var(--burn) ± n%), wash stops the same, hotspots at calc(100% - var(--burn)), embers spawn at bottom: calc(var(--burn) + n%).
continuous tick — recompute heat every 30s and set --burn; the CSS transition (2.4s ease-out) absorbs the step so nothing visibly jumps.
tier crossing — 600ms front sweep, cubic-bezier(.4,0,.2,1), with a one-shot bloom flare to 1.15× settling over 400ms. Fires only while the app is open.
new note — enters at its true --burn, no burn-in animation; only position animates (FLIP, 260ms).
snuff — --burn animates to 0% over 220ms while the note desaturates, then it slides out over 280ms.
reduced motion — --burn is set, never transitioned; embers, flare and hotspot pulses are off. The stage still reads because size, front position and countdown all carry it.

How heat is carried
bloom (from 4D) — 0 0 (0→46px) (0→10px) rgba(226,78,27, heat×.5); overdue adds an outer radial plate at inset -16px, blur 6px, 3.4s pulse. Peripheral detection lives here.

corner char (from 4C) — radial from 100% 100%, size = heat × 78% of the square. This is the progress read: how much corner is gone.
tongues — 1 at Burn, 2 at Engulfed, anchored at the consumed corner, screen blend, 10–12px past the edge. No tongues below heat .78.
paper (from 3A) — stays #EDEAE2 → #FBFAF6, brightening as it heats; body ink never leaves #191512, meta never leaves #5C544C.
countdown — bottom-right while cold, moves bottom-left from Kindle up, ahead of the corner char.
layout — 160px squares, 24px gap so tongues and bloom never touch a neighbour; no panel, no header, entry box only.
Pyre · four more ideas · v0.4
Four further ways to carry heat on a borderless grid, no panel in any of them. 4A is the yellowing idea: paper ages on one clock and burns on another, so a note can be old-and-cold or fresh-and-on-fire. Each option shows four states.
4A
Yellow, then burn
Two clocks. Notes are born white and yellow with age — days since created, edge-in. Burning is driven separately by due. An old undated note looks like forgotten newsprint; a fresh urgent one is white paper on fire.
